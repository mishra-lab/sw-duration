\section{Application}\label{app}
We implemented the above mathematical relationship in a Bayesian model,
allowing us to infer
the distribution of total durations among the source population $f(x)$
from individual-level and/or aggregate data on
the distribution of censored durations among the sampled population $f(z|s)$.
Specifically, we focused on the types of aggregate estimates reviewed by \citet{Fazito2012}:
duration means $\sE[z|s]$, and
proportions of durations within a given interval $[a,b]$, denoted $\sP[\azb|s]$
--- \eg if 33\% of durations were between 2 and 5 years, then $\sP[\lzu25|s] = 0.33$ ---
which can also encompass estimated medians, quartiles, etc. with $a=0$.

\subsection{Bayesian Model}
The model assumes a parametric distribution for
total durations among the source population $f(x)$.
Specifically, we considered 6 candidate distribution families:
Exponential, Gamma, Weibull, Log-Normal, Scaled Beta, and Scaled Kumaraswamy,
all truncated or scaled to 50 years maximum duration (Table~\ref{tab:fams}).
For convenience, we denote the parameters of all families as: $\aa,\bb$
(for Exponential: $\bb$ only),
so the interpretation of and priors for each parameter depend on the family.
\par
We modelled the probability of
being sampled for survey participation among the active population as
increasing exponentially from $\pp \in [0.5,1]$ initially
to 95\% by year $\tt \in [0,3]$, and 100\% asymptotically:%
\footnote{Thus, if either $\pp = 1$ or $\tt = 0$,
  then sampling will be modelled as
  perfectly representative of the active population:
  $f(s|z) = 1$ and so $f(z|s) = f(z|a)$.}
\begin{equation}
  f(s|z) = 1 + \big(\pp-1\big)
    e^{\bigg(\frac{z}{\tt}\log\bigg(\frac{0.05}{1-\pp}\bigg)\bigg)}
  \label{eq:f(s|z)}
\end{equation}
For reference, Figure~\ref{fig:diag.psz} illustrates this sampling model graphically.
We then defined the distribution of censored durations among the sampled population
$f(z|s;\aa,\bb,\pp,\tt)$ via \eqref{eq:f(z|s)}.
Finally, \eqrefa{eq:lE}{eq:lP} define the likelihood functions for
estimates $\sE_i[z|s;N_i]$ and $\sP_i[\azb|s;N_i]$
from empirical data with sample size $N_i$.
Similar functions could be specified for individual-level data.
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Implementation}
We implemented the model using Stan~\cite{Stan2024},
with default random parameter initialization and sampling algorithm~\cite{Betancourt2018}.
For a given set of empirical data
--- \ie aggregate estimate(s) of $\sE_i[z|s;N_i]$ and/or $\sP_i[\azb|s;N_i]$ ---
we fit the model separately under each candidate family,
and then selected the best-fitting family (highest total likelihood).
We retained 1000 consecutive samples after 500 warm-up iterations from 7 chains,
confirming convergence visually through parameter trace plots
(\eg Figure~\ref{fig:demo.trace}).%
\footnote{We manually removed 3 chains that did not converge.}
This approach yielded 7000 posterior parameter samples, from which we computed
the distribution of total durations among the source population $f(x;\aa,\bb)$,
and characteristics thereof, \eg the mean $\E[x;\aa,\bb]$,
each with uncertainty.

The best-fitting family was Gamma.
We find that the model can reproduce the empirical proportion data well
(Figure~\ref{fig:demo.props}.i),
but may suffer from unidentifiability using these data alone --- \ie
many combinations of parameters can reproduce the data equally well.
For example, posterior uncertainty was
low around the observed duration distribution $f(z|s)$, but
high around the inferred "true" duration distribution $f(x)$
(Figure~\ref{fig:demo.props}.ii, colour vs gray).
Similarly, posterior \CI were relatively
narrow for $\E[z|s]$: 4.37--5.83 and $\CV[z|s]$: 0.72--1.15, but
wide for $\E[x]$: 2.30--8.53 and $\CV[x]$: 0.43--1.79
(Figure~\ref{fig:demo.pars}).
Posteriors for $\E[z|s]$ and $\CV[z|s]$ also remained similar across families,
whereas posteriors for $\E[x]$ and $\CV[x]$ varied even further across families
(Figure~\ref{fig:demo.pars}).

regionally pooled estimates of mean total duration in sex work
using the same aggregate data and region definitions as \citet{Fazito2012},
and compared our pooled estimates to theirs.%
